GPD RECONSTRUCTION PROGRAM: PERSPECTIVE AFTER P2A PRESERVATION
26 October 2026

The reconstruction framework retains substantial positive evidence. P2A closes
one unsuccessful attempt to stabilize its noisy inverse. The program now needs
a better justified rule for selecting GPD information from the freedom left by
the observations, with accuracy tested beyond the fitted loss. The latest result
is particularly informative because the added penalty damages a clean benchmark
that the existing procedure already reconstructs well.

This assessment reviews the original scientific objective, principal completed
branches, relevant fitting/model/regularizer/selection code, and reported numerical
evidence. It is a synthesis of the existing program, not a fresh execution of every
historical verification or a full line-by-line software audit. The immediately
preceding preservation review independently replayed its saved fields. No new fit,
noise draw, operator build, cluster operation or manuscript edit accompanies this
synthesis. SOURCES.json records the consulted documents and code with hashes;
EVIDENCE.json records the checked comparison values and arithmetic.

The original purpose and the method that has survived

The aim is to reconstruct a nontrivial reference-scale charge-weighted H+ field
from CFF information, demonstrate the method on controlled examples, and support
a quantitatively qualified experimental interpretation. The intended construction
is flexible FiLM inference, unknown-coefficient polynomiality, symbolic hypothesis
generation and refitting through the observables. A unique unrestricted inverse
was never a defensible finish line. Neither was a collection of procedural passes
without useful reconstruction. [S01-S03]

The current reference network has 3,867 parameters. It learns three stored fields
F=(Sigma,T_rescaled,Hg=xg), with H+=(2/9)(Sigma+T_rescaled), at Q0^2=2 GeV^2.
It conditions on xi and t; Q2 dependence comes from the fixed coupled NLO forward
map, not a learned Q2 input. Odd/even projections impose channel parity, and an
(1-x^2)^2 envelope imposes endpoints. At spins 2,4,6,8 the moment penalty measures
departure from the complete allowed polynomial spaces, retaining unknown allowed
coefficients. These are finite-spin, approximate conditions. The neural envelope,
finite capacity, collocation, numerical domain and finite optimizer budget are
also substantive assumptions. The baseline already selects among alternatives;
lambda_stab=0 does not mean an assumption-free or unregularized inverse in every
sense. [S04-S07]

The production real-input chain and the separate upstream experiments concern two
inverse problems:

  measured cross sections -> inferred ReH information -> reconstructed H+

The intermediate CFF values are themselves estimates with bias, correlation and
method dependence. Better cross-section RMS can coexist with worse ReH; better
ReH matching can coexist with worse H+. Numerical precision in the forward map
addresses another question again. This distinction organizes the whole record.

What the completed branches establish

1. The forward physics and neural interface have a validated numerical foundation.

The accepted NLO milestone implements coupled singlet/gluon and nonsinglet
evolution, two-loop running and the specified hard coefficients in fixed-nf=3
MSbar. Point references, weak/strong discretizations, hard integration, moments,
time ordering and derivative checks were separately exercised. Subsequent saved-
field audits reproduce the intended contractions and derivatives. The reference
discretizations share kernel sources, so this is bounded numerical validation,
not independent certification by an unrelated full NLO library or a rigorous
continuum error bound. Early failed source/portability checks are historical;
later acceptance must be read in sequence. [S08-S09]

The matched noiseless neural controls achieved small charge-field errors, and
the symbolic/refit sequence was actually demonstrated. Yet compatible symbolic
D-term proposals omitted eight known nonzero coefficients. Symbolic compactness
therefore supplies interpretable alternatives, with no license to call omitted
terms physical zeros. That remains a positive integration result and a measured
resolution limitation. [S09-S10]

2. Useful field recovery exists, including with real-only input.

The t-dependent coarse complex-CFF demonstration achieved 4.90% H+ error on its
declared checking chart. It used one Z1 truth and seed; denser L1/L2 calculations
were paused. Sampling convergence and uniform accuracy have not been measured.
At the 160 actual experimental coordinates, exact real-only inputs gave wider-
chart H+ errors 0.75% and 3.05% for seeds 31 and 43. Under one frozen full-noise
perturbation those became 32.69% and 49.21%. Exact input is thus a meaningful
capacity/recovery control. The different complex and real-only geometries and
weights prevent attributing their difference solely to the presence of ImH or
to measurement density. [S03,S11-S12]

Successful H+ recovery does not establish equally good identification of every
internal channel. In the coarse complex-CFF case, the 4.90% charge-field error
coexisted with Sigma/T_rescaled/Hg errors of 78.46%/235.01%/5.90% on that chart.
This matters for P2A: its norm penalizes all three stored fields, including
combinations not demonstrated to be separately well determined. [S11,S33]

P1 made a more controlled precision comparison: same 160 rows, original sigmas,
one noise direction and fresh seed31; only its perturbation amplitude changed.

 Perturbation amplitude     Observed-slice H+ error     Wider-chart H+ error
 0                                  0.92%                      0.75%
 0.25                               7.81%                     18.46%
 0.50                              11.46%                     28.61%
 1                                 16.53%                     32.69%

At quarter amplitude, target A/B signed errors were +1.49%/-1.01%; at half
amplitude, -4.99%/-1.28%. Useful target recovery can coexist with larger whole-
field error. Input ReH norm-relative errors of 31.78% and 63.56% at those two
amplitudes became fitted ReH truth errors of 2.53% and 4.15%. The existing method
already suppresses much of this particular input perturbation. A new stabilizer
has to improve that capability, not assume none exists. These are sensitivity
results for one truth/noise/seed, not an experimental precision requirement.
Observed-slice and wider-chart norms have different support and weights. [S13]

Here and in P2A, target A is 5*integral_[0.2,0.4] H+(x) dx and target B is
integral_[0,1] x*H+(x) dx, both at xi=0.275, t=-0.363 and the reference scale.
Their A/B labels must not be confused with the two truth strata named A/B in
the earlier calibration campaign. [S13,S16]

3. A finite-budget procedure can be calibrated for specified targets, even while
   its optimizer is nonstationary.

The completed, prospectively amended 442-case A/B study used 160 calibration and
282 independent test observations. Joint coverage was 135/141 (95.74%) in A and
131/141 (92.91%) in B, with a common absolute target half-width 0.01858363.
It retained finite structural/numerical flags in the accounting and calibrated
the actual fixed procedure. This is a substantive uncertainty result. It covers
two targets under two fixed synthetic truths and a specified observation law.
It does not calibrate the later t-dependent Z1 field, a full-function band, P2A,
or experimental inference. Small target values in stratum A also make those
finite intervals broad in relative terms. The 198 omitted tests were excluded
prospectively, not failed or unfinished mandatory work. [S14-S16]

4. The experimental central product is conditional and its propagation is unstable
   over the budgets examined.

The source audit excluded a malformed legacy merge and a raw-k=0 extrapolated
surface. The later author-designated mixed CDNN/FQDNN ReH source is the settled
input; the separate effective H-dominance Re/Im product must not be substituted
for it. Original quoted combined errors are used once. [S17-S18]

The nominated thirteen-t central field has ReH RMS residual 2.4335 in quoted-
error units, with 30/160 absolute pulls above three. Those are weighted residual
diagnostics, not goodness-of-fit probabilities under an established correlated
likelihood. The thirteen-t change improved sampled moment consistency relative
to its equal-iteration control while modestly worsening ReH agreement. Later
smoothing and mean-target continuations reduced some residuals without settling
the mismatch. [S18-S21]

The 32-member CFF cohort is complete; only two members were propagated in the
matched GPD budget comparison. From cumulative steps 5 to 20, the mean CFF RMS
improved by 4.54%, while one member-minus-mean GPD response norm grew 4.803-fold.
Changes from steps 10 to 20 were still substantial. A narrow early response is
therefore not evidence of precise experimental determination. More members alone
would estimate this chosen finite algorithm's distribution more thoroughly;
they would not settle its bias or budget dependence. [S22-S23]

The numerical-boundary probes identify a real interaction with the optimizer:
its private field-discretization margin is 0.00095 versus an outer reporting
guide of 0.001. Several rejected trials were below the outer guide. The composite
checks find both norm-ratio curvature and neural nonlinearity. They do not prove
a derivative bug or quantify how much experimental mismatch a different solver
would remove. Relaxing numerical checks is not justified by these probes alone.
[S24-S25]

5. The data have strongly and weakly measured directions, independently of the solver.

The constructed D-term pair differs by 25% in the specified field norm but has
complete-vector whitened ReH separation d=0.558809 under the declared Gaussian
law. Even optimal binary identification succeeds only 61.00% on average. At the
same field separation, another direction in the tested two-dimensional span
has d=42.53. This establishes directional information limits, alongside strongly
measured information. It does not make every reconstruction impossible or explain
the full error of the current estimator. [S26-S27]

Those alternatives share forward distributions and their first relevant moment.
Forward PDF information cannot distinguish that particular pair. Additional
constraints can still improve other directions. The result is conditional on
the structural benchmark, finite maps and stated covariance; full QCD physical
admissibility or compatibility with the real experimental central values was
not proved for the pair.

6. Upstream improvement is possible, but its usefulness must be checked downstream.

Hard reflection improved cross-section truth RMS over a matched unconstrained
surrogate but barely improved ReH; both surrogates were worse than direct KMI.
The response-space diagnosis showed much of the cleaned-up error lay outside
the local CFF response. More exact head fitting of those surrogate outputs
could lower the objective and greatly worsen ReH. [S28]

A subsequent frozen physics-decoded/profiling/stabilization candidate reduced
ReH RMSE by 29.18%/39.27% on fresh noise at the two existing Z1/Z2 truths, winning
eight of eight paired draws per truth. Bias and empirical variation both fell.
This is positive independent-noise evidence for that upstream procedure, with
remaining bias and no calibrated band or direct validation of the publication's
production formulation. It changes multiple method components, so the gain cannot
be attributed solely to symmetry or optimization. [S29-S30]

The downstream twelve-coordinate test then gave H+ input-slice errors of 1.58%
for exact ReH, 293.50% for direct KMI and 184.75% for the improved candidate.
The candidate helped this triplet but remained far from accurate, including
wrong-sign target integrals. Near-exact fitting of biased CFF inputs did not
recover the true field. This is a different observation geometry/law from P1,
not another point on its precision curve. [S31]

What P2A adds to this record

P2A's purpose was reasonable: explicitly stabilize a selection procedure whose
loss could improve while truth error worsened. The tested choice, however,
was a particular zero-centered field norm, not a generic test of all smoothing:

 R = (1/(3N)) sum_i sum_c integral_0^1
       [F_c(x,xi_i,t_i)^2 + 0.10^2 (partial_x F_c)^2] dx,

with split integration at x=xi, original observation multiplicities, N=160 and
equal stored-channel scales. It penalizes amplitudes and first derivatives in
three internal channels; it contains no PDF anchor, no xi/t derivative term and
no direct information about the true field. The equal channel scales and fixed
physical-x length define a substantive preference. The implementation retains
the full residual block and its mixed derivatives, rather than replacing its
Gauss-Newton geometry by one scalar residual. [S32-S34]

The development outcome was already weak: low moved observed H+ error from
11.46345% to 11.16003%, a 0.30342 percentage-point gain, while A error worsened
from -4.99% to +10.23%. Wider-chart error improved from 28.61% to 18.37%. High
gave 26.20090% observed error. Low won the declared numerical criterion; a
numerical winner was never evidence of a practically robust gain. No independent
noise assessment was reached. [S35]

Preservation used that unchanged low lambda=0.03694325396955194 on the original
exact input and fresh seed31. Its observed H+ error is 17.10372%, with A/B absolute
errors 5.90349% and 14.75430%, versus baseline 0.92029%, 0.48477%, 0.86910%.
Every frozen 2% gate fails. The completed continuation and selected stage remain
valid recorded outcomes; their finite-budget eligibility is distinct from
convergence. [S36-S37]

The most useful interpretation goes beyond a threshold failure. Across saved
stages 20/40/60, observed error rises 2.94% -> 7.31% -> 17.10% as J_lambda falls
0.0188372 -> 0.0153005 -> 0.0133424. Using the exact baseline's recorded J_P1
and independently replayed R gives its value under this same penalized objective:

 state                         H+ observed error       J_lambda
 accurate exact baseline             0.9203%           0.02199759
 selected preservation field        17.1037%           0.01334240

The frozen objective ranks the degraded field above this accurate baseline.
That is direct two-state evidence that this preference conflicts with the desired
clean reconstruction. It does not require a convergence claim. It also does not
prove the unique cause of the trajectory, a global optimum, or that every lower
objective field would be inaccurate. The 90.8% scalar penalty share is not a
parameter-gradient or causal decomposition.

There is an important qualification: the exact-input test retained the original
nonzero sigmas, and lambda was calibrated in scale to a half-noise development
case. An estimator designed to trade bias against noise at those fixed weights
can remain biased when handed clean targets. The preservation rule deliberately
required this candidate also to retain good clean recovery. Its failure rejects
that combined design requirement. It does not mathematically prove that the
penalty cannot improve expected noisy error somewhere. The independent data
needed to establish such a benefit were never run, and the mixed development
result alone does not establish it.

This finding strengthens a program-wide conclusion: additional minimization of
an objective whose ranking is misaligned with reconstruction accuracy is not,
by itself, a route to accurate H+. Earlier solver limitations remain real, but
they cannot serve as a blanket explanation or a reason to extend every run.
Likewise, the information-limit calculation cannot explain away degradation of
this specific recoverable clean benchmark. Separate information, objective
preference, representation and finite optimization in future decisions.

The next scientific direction

My recommendation is to retain the original flexible NLO/FiLM/free-moment
framework and the useful baseline, close this P2A candidate, and give priority
to the already planned P2B information/compatibility design. This is a proposed
research priority, not an executed new comparison or permission to release fits.

P2B asks a different question: how much uncertainty-bearing forward information
improves inference. First establish a compatible NLO/MSbar/fixed-nf=3 scale and
channel mapping, a coherent uncertainty model, common CFF/PDF loss normalization,
and a finite x interval the existing network can represent. The current odd
quark network vanishes at x=0, and t=0 lies outside its observed negative-t nodes;
endpoint and boundary behavior require an explicit check. A dense PDF grid is
not a set of independent new measurements. [S38]

The cleanest first controlled design uses a forward input consistent with the
CFF-generating benchmark, with stated uncertainty. Real proton PDFs instead
require a compatible benchmark; attaching them to unrelated Z1 CFFs would mix
input inconsistency with method performance. Retain baseline versus PDF-only
as the clean primary contrast. The failed P2A penalty is not automatically a
beneficial ingredient in a combined arm. Forward constraints may anchor some
channel/amplitude freedom, but the same-forward D-term pair sets a clear limit
on what they can resolve.

Any new stabilization proposal also needs a declared relationship between its
strength, input precision and desired targets before more expensive full fits.
Inspecting its ranking on existing accurate/degraded states is a useful cheap
screen, though not validation of fresh optimization. Amplitude shrinkage,
derivative smoothing, channel normalization and additional physical information
should be separately interpretable. A derivative-only norm, smaller lambda,
different architecture or altered solver is a hypothesis, not a remedy already
established by this failure. Do not select stage 20/40 or another lambda
retrospectively and reuse the old validation claim.

The original exact-preservation and independent-assessment logic remains useful.
New methods need matched exact/noisy controls, common supports/weights, separately
reported field and A/B accuracy, and an independent assessment after selection.
Observable holdouts test predictions; the existing weak-direction example means
they cannot alone certify all H+ shapes. Further truth/seed/coverage expansion
should follow a scientifically credible candidate, rather than enlarge a
campaign whose basic ranking problem remains unresolved.

The program's defensible endpoint is already clearer

There is a substantial methods result: flexible, physics-coupled reconstruction
works on controlled inputs; symbolic simplification and selected-target calibration
work at bounded scopes; data quality and direction matter; estimator choices can
introduce large bias; and some off-forward information is intrinsically weak
under the stated observation law. Positive upstream improvement shows avoidable
error exists. Failed P2A preservation shows an intuitively plausible downstream
regularizer can lose a capability the baseline already possessed.

The stronger experimental goal remains unfinished: a robust central reconstruction
with justified total H+ uncertainty. The current central curve, method sensitivity,
coherent input spread and finite-budget response are distinct evidence products.
The synthetic A/B cutoff, P1 errors, two-member spread and P2A outcome cannot be
combined into an experimental band. No manuscript work was initiated here.

The appropriate perspective is to consolidate those established achievements and
make the remaining estimator/information question explicit. P2A was a useful
negative experiment that narrows the next design; it does not reset the program
to numerical construction or erase its successful closures.
